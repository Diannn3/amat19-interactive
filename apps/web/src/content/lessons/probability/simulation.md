A theoretical probability describes the mathematical model. An empirical frequency is what happened in a finite experiment.

If an event has theoretical probability `p`, a long seeded simulation may produce a cumulative success ratio close to `p`, but the experiment does not *prove* the probability. The exact model and the random experiment answer different questions.

The simulation lab deliberately exposes its seed so the same experiment can be reproduced, discussed, and debugged.
