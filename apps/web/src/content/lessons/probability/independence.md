Events A and B are independent when learning that one occurred does not change the probability of the other.

Equivalent checks include

`P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B)`

and, when the relevant denominators are nonzero,

`P(A|B) = P(A)`.

Independence is different from mutual exclusivity. If two positive-probability events are mutually exclusive, they cannot occur together, so their intersection probability is zero rather than the positive product `P(A)P(B)`.

Use exact fractions when testing independence so two rounded decimals do not appear equal by accident.
